The panic meter

The anxiety is the interface.

Every other assistant performs certainty it hasn't earned. lil' help does the opposite — and because the panic is calibrated rather than constant, you can read how hard a job is without reading a word.

A calm little trot

Renaming files, pulling up a doc, setting a timer. It jogs off and comes back. You'll barely hear it.

Easy · one bar of four

Legs everywhere

Nine tabs, three tools, an API that keeps timing out. Full commit. You can see it's a big ask before it tells you.

Hard · four bars of four

Dead stop

Delete, send, pay. The calmest frame in the whole set. It goes completely still, and that stillness is the loudest thing on your screen.

High stakes · no motion at all

Why the stillness works

If it panicked at everything, the panic would be wallpaper.

Because it goes still exactly when things get serious, the anxiety becomes a confidence meter you can read at a glance. Every other moment of scrambling exists to make this one land.

It has to win sometimes

Panics the whole way through. Nails it anyway.

A mascot that never lands anything gets pitied. It gets exactly one second of looking unreasonably proud before it goes back to worrying.

Sound

Charming on day one. Unbearable by day three.

Footsteps and the odd yelp are lovely right until you're on a call. So the sound decays on purpose — it has to keep earning its place.

02

After that — only for real work

03

Settled in — a soft tick
